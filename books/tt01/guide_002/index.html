PUZZLE #002: THE START

The Start

Puzzle #002 from page 10 of The Classic Train Track Logic Puzzle Book by Peter Kelby.

Start by scanning columns A to F, then rows 1 to 6. Continue to repeat those scans as the clues start to emerge.

Remember

  • A dot means the square must have track.
  • X means the square is empty.
  • Draw a simple line to mark the track once you figure out the direction.

The small letters and row numbers in the diagrams identify squares. B2 means column B, row 2.

Each diagram shows the position after the instructions beside it.

Step 9

Rows 2 to 4

  • There are no new clues

Row 5

  • A5 only has one possible connection, so it can't be a track square – mark it with an X.
  • We need 3 track squares so C5 must be track – mark it with a dot.
  • C5 cannot connect down or left, so it must connect C4 and D5 – draw in the track.
  • D5 now has 2 connections, so draw in the horizontal track between C5 and E5.

Step 10

Column A

  • We need 4 track squares so mark A2 with a dot.
  • A2 cannot connect up or left so it must connect B2 and A3 – draw in the track.
  • A4 can only connect A3 and B4 (because of the grid edge and the X in A5) – draw in the track.
  • A3 now has 2 connections, so draw in the vertical track between A2 and A4.

Column B

  • B2 now has 2 connections – draw in the track joining B1 and A2.
  • B3 cannot connect up or left because those track pieces already have their two connections. It must connect C3 and B4 – draw in the track.
  • B4 now has 2 connections – draw in the track joining B3 and A4.

Step 11

Column C

  • We have 4 track squares allocated so mark C2 with an X.
  • C3 now has 2 connections, so draw in the horizontal track between B3 and D3.
  • C4 cannot connect up or left because those track pieces already have their two connections. It must connect D4 and C5 – draw in the track.

Column D

  • D4 now has 2 connections – draw in the track joining D3 and C4.

The Completed Route

Congratulations!

You’ve traced the track from one arrow to the other. Every printed piece is used in one continuous route, with no branches, crossings or separate loops.
